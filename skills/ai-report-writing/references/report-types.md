# Choose structure by purpose

Use the user's required template first. These are starting points, not mandatory headings.

| Report type | Reader's main need | Useful content | Do not invent |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Research or academic | Understand the question, method, result, and limits | Context, related work, method as performed, results, interpretation, limitations | Data, statistical tests, approvals, novelty claims |
| Business or decision memo | Make a supported decision | Decision needed, options, evidence, assumptions, tradeoffs, recommendation | Revenue, costs, stakeholder agreement, promised outcomes |
| Technical or handover | Understand, operate, or continue the work | Scope, architecture, versions, interfaces, observed checks, remaining work | Implemented features, test passes, operational acceptance |
| Progress or operational | Know status and act on deviations | Reporting period, actual progress, comparable baseline, blockers, next actions | Completion, owners, deadlines, commitments |
| Incident or evaluation | Understand what happened and what can be concluded | Timeline, observations, hypotheses, impact, evidence, corrective actions | Root cause before isolation, blame, effectiveness before verification |
| Educational or analytical | Follow a reasoned explanation | Question, concepts, worked reasoning, evidence, interpretation | Learning outcomes or evaluations not actually measured |

For a short report, combine sections rather than adding empty headings. For a long report, assign each section a distinct role and use references instead of repeating background.

A conclusion should answer the stated question within the evidence. An action plan should distinguish suggestions from assigned or approved work. A research abstract and a business executive summary have different functions; neither should introduce unsupported facts.
